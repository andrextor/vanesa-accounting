It’s the question I get most on WhatsApp: *“If someone sends me money through Nequi, do I have to file?”*. The short answer: **there is no special threshold for Nequi, Daviplata or Bre-B**. What you receive through them adds up toward the usual thresholds. Here is the long answer.

## For the DIAN, a digital wallet is just like a bank account

Nequi, Daviplata, Dale and other wallets are deposits held by regulated entities, and **Bre-B** is the Central Bank’s (Banco de la República) instant payment system, which connects banks and wallets through keys (phone number, ID, email or a code). For income tax purposes, everything that comes into those accounts counts the same as a deposit at a bank.

Financial institutions report information about their customers to the DIAN, which cross-checks it against what you file. It doesn’t matter whether the money arrived through a regular transfer, PSE, Bre-B or a wallet: **it all adds up**.

## When does it make me file in 2027?

The criteria are the same for every individual. For the return filed in 2027 (tax year 2026), with the COP 52,374 UVT:

- **Deposits and investments** over 1,400 UVT (**COP 73,323,600**) during the year, adding up all your accounts and wallets.
- **Gross income** of 1,400 UVT or more.
- And the other criteria: assets, credit card and purchases.

So if in 2026 more than COP 73.3 million came in across Nequi, Daviplata and your bank accounts, **you must file**, even if not all of that money was income. Check every criterion in [who must file an income tax return in 2027](/en/blog/quien-debe-declarar-renta/).

## Receiving money isn’t always income

Being required to file is one thing; paying tax is another. On your return you only pay tax on your **income**, and not everything transferred to you is income:

- **Loans** made to you or paid back to you.
- **Reimbursements**, for example when a friend pays you their share of a bill.
- **Money between your own accounts**, such as moving money from the bank to Nequi.
- **Money you receive on behalf of someone else**, such as what you collect for a group gift.

The catch is that the DIAN doesn’t know what each transfer is. So if your account activity is high, **keep records**: a loan agreement, a message, a receipt. If the DIAN asks, they back you up.

## If you sell or get paid through Nequi or Bre-B

If you run a business or work independently and clients pay you through these platforms, **that is income**, just as if they paid you in cash. It must be on your return and, if you are required to, on your electronic invoices. Getting paid through Nequi doesn’t make income invisible: if anything, it leaves a trail.

## What about the 1.5% withholding?

In late 2025, the Government published a draft decree to apply a 1.5% withholding tax on electronic payments to merchants, including Bre-B and digital wallets. After receiving more than 170 comments, the Ministry of Finance announced in November 2025 that **withholding on these payments would be 0%**.

Besides, the draft never sought to tax transfers between people: only payments that were income from selling goods or services. **Sending money to a relative or a friend doesn’t trigger any tax.**

## What’s coming in 2027

- **Bre-B for businesses.** The Central Bank expects Bre-B to be used for payroll, supplier payments and collections progressively from 2027, although the regulation isn’t final yet. More digital payments means more information for the DIAN, so keeping your books in order will matter even more.
- **Economic Rescue bill.** In October 2026 the Government filed a bill that, among other things, would change the income tax table for individuals. **It has not been approved** and, if approved, it would apply from tax year 2027 (the return filed in 2028). **It doesn’t change the return you file in 2027.** If it moves forward, I’ll update this article.
