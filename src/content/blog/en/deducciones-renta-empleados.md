Many employees file their income tax return without using the benefits the law gives them, and end up paying more than they need to. If you are on a salary, these are the ones with the biggest impact. Peso amounts use the 2026 UVT (COP 52,374), which applies to the return you file in 2027.

## The general cap: 40% and 1,340 UVT

Almost all deductions and exempt income share a cap: together they can’t exceed **40% of your income** (after subtracting non-taxable income) or **1,340 UVT per year (COP 70,181,160)**. So it’s not enough to add up benefits: you need to see which ones fit under the cap and which ones sit outside it.

## Under the cap

- **25% exempt labor income.** It applies to what is left of your salary after the other deductions, capped at 790 UVT per year (COP 41,375,460).
- **Mortgage interest** or housing lease: up to 100 UVT per month (COP 5,237,400).
- **Private health plans** or health insurance: up to 16 UVT per month (COP 837,984).
- **Dependents:** 10% of your labor income, up to 32 UVT per month (COP 1,675,968).
- **Voluntary pension and AFC contributions:** up to 30% of your income, capped at 3,800 UVT per year (COP 199,021,200), provided you meet the holding requirements.

## Outside the cap

These benefits are added separately, even if you have already reached the 40% cap:

- **72 UVT per dependent (COP 3,770,928)**, for up to four dependents. It’s on top of the 10% in the previous list, although you can’t use the same dependent for both benefits.
- **1% of your purchases with electronic invoices**, up to 240 UVT (COP 12,569,760), as long as you paid electronically and didn’t use them as a cost or deduction elsewhere.

## An example

Paula earns COP 8,000,000 a month and has a child. She pays a mortgage and a private health plan. If she only reports her salary, she pays tax on almost all of it. If she includes the mortgage interest, the health plan, her child as a dependent and her electronic invoice purchases, the base she pays tax on drops by several million pesos, and so does her tax.

Every case changes depending on income and records. What matters is having the certificates: without a record, the deduction doesn’t count.
