Living outside Colombia doesn’t automatically free you from the DIAN, but it doesn’t mean reporting everything twice either. The key is your **tax residency** during 2026, the year reported in 2027.

## Step 1: are you still a Colombian tax resident?

Nationality doesn’t determine the obligation: tax residency does. In general, you are a Colombian tax resident if:

- You were in the country **for more than 183 days**, consecutive or not, within a 365-day period.
- Or, being Colombian, your **spouse or minor children** live in Colombia, or most of your **income or assets** are here.

There is an important exception: if you are Colombian and at least half of your income comes from the country where you live, or half of your assets are there, you may stop being a Colombian tax resident. This point is analyzed case by case.

## If you are a tax resident

You file like anyone in Colombia, with the same [filing thresholds](/en/blog/topes-para-declarar-renta/), but you include **everything you earn and own worldwide**: your salary abroad, your accounts and your properties outside the country.

Does that mean paying tax twice? Not necessarily:

- **Credit for taxes paid abroad.** Income tax you already paid in the other country can be credited against Colombian tax, within certain limits.
- **Double taxation treaties.** Colombia has a treaty with **Spain**, which defines which country taxes each type of income. There is no treaty with the **United States**, so only the credit applies.

Also, if your assets abroad exceed a certain value, you must file the **foreign assets return** (declaración de activos en el exterior), which is a separate form.

## If you are not a tax resident

You only report your **Colombian-source** income in Colombia: for example, rent from an apartment in Cali or the sale of a property in the country. If that income already had the full withholding applied, in many cases you don’t have to file.

## Can I file without traveling to Colombia?

Yes. The return is filed online on the DIAN portal, with the **electronic signature** you activate on the portal itself. The whole process, from gathering documents to paying, can be done from abroad. That’s how I work with clients living in the United States and Spain.

## What people forget most

- **Updating the RUT** with your address abroad and your current email, so you receive DIAN notifications.
- **Bank accounts in Colombia** that are still open: their deposits count toward the thresholds.
- **Assets in Colombia**: a house in your name may require you to file even if you no longer live here.
