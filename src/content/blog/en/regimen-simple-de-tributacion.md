The **Simple Tax Regime (Régimen Simple de Tributación, RST)** was created so small businesses could pay their taxes more easily. For many it’s a good option; for others it ends up costing more than the ordinary regime. Here is how to tell which case is yours.

## What it is

It’s a unified tax that **replaces ordinary-regime income tax** and **includes consolidated ICA** (the municipal industry and commerce tax, signage tax and fire surcharge, as applicable). It can also include the national consumption tax for specific activities. **It doesn’t remove VAT obligations** if you are registered for VAT. It’s paid through bimonthly advance payments and a consolidated annual return. The rate is applied to **gross income**, depending on the business activity and its income level.

## Who can join

- **Individuals** who run a business or work independently in a profession.
- **Companies** whose partners are individuals.

In both cases, ordinary and extraordinary gross income for the previous year must be **below 100,000 UVT**. To apply in 2027, 2026 income is what counts: COP 5,237,400,000 with the 2026 UVT. You also need to check the special rules for professional activities and the other requirements of article 905 of the Estatuto Tributario.

Some activities can’t join, such as financial advisory or factoring. It also doesn’t apply to someone who, in practice, has an employment relationship with their client.

## Advantages

- **No income tax withholding** on your Simple Regime income, and you don’t withhold income tax either, except on payroll payments. This doesn’t remove VAT withholding where it legally applies.
- **A unified tax** instead of separate income tax and ICA, with less paperwork.
- **Credits:** the pension contributions you pay as an employer and part of the income you receive through card or electronic payments can be credited against the tax.

One important obligation: under the Simple Regime you must issue **electronic invoices**.

## When it doesn’t pay off

Because the tax is calculated mainly on **gross income** and costs and expenses aren’t subtracted, the Simple Regime can be expensive if your business has **high costs and low margins**. For example, in a shop that sells a lot but earns little per product, paying a percentage of everything it sells may exceed what it would pay in income tax.

Nor can you assume it always suits independent professionals. You need to compare rates, income, costs, ICA, contributions and credits against the ordinary regime.

## What’s the deadline to switch?

If you already have a RUT, you can opt into the Simple Regime between January 1 and the **last business day of February** of the year you want it to apply: for 2027, until the last business day of February 2027. If you are a new taxpayer, you can join when you register in the RUT, as long as you meet the legal requirements. That’s why November and December are a good time to run the numbers.
