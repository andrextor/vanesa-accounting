The DIAN sets its thresholds in **UVT** (Unidad de Valor Tributario), a tax value unit that changes every year. For the return filed in 2027, for tax year 2026, the UVT is **COP 52,374**. Here are the thresholds in pesos.

## Thresholds for filing in 2027

| Criterion in 2026 | Threshold | In pesos (COP) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gross assets on December 31 | Over 4,500 UVT | 235,683,000 |
| Gross income | 1,400 UVT or more | 73,323,600 |
| Credit card spending | Over 1,400 UVT | 73,323,600 |
| Total purchases and spending | Over 1,400 UVT | 73,323,600 |
| Deposits and investments | Over 1,400 UVT | 73,323,600 |

Going over **one** of these thresholds is enough to be required to file. To understand each criterion, read [who must file an income tax return in 2027](/en/blog/quien-debe-declarar-renta/).

## Is there a monthly threshold?

**No.** The 1,400 UVT threshold applies to gross income for the whole tax year, not month by month. Dividing COP 73,323,600 by 12 gives about COP 6,110,300, but that figure is only an arithmetic average, not a limit: what counts is the sum of your 2026 income (salary, fees, rent and other income).

## What counts toward each threshold

- **Gross assets:** everything in your name (real estate, vehicles, accounts, investments, shares) **without subtracting debts**. The tax value is used, which doesn’t always match the market value. Real estate has specific rules (article 277 of the Estatuto Tributario): in general terms, those required to keep books use the tax cost, and those who aren’t may report it at the higher of its acquisition cost and its cadastral appraisal, taking into account the applicable tax cost and adjustment rules.
- **Gross income:** everything you received during the year before deductions: salary, fees, rent, interest, dividends, pensions, among others.
- **Credit cards:** the sum of everything you paid with your credit cards.
- **Purchases and spending:** everything you spent during the year, with any payment method.
- **Deposits:** everything that came into your accounts, even if it isn’t income. A loan or money from a relative also counts, and so does what you receive through Nequi, Daviplata or Bre-B ([I explain it here](/en/blog/nequi-bre-b-declarar-renta/)).

## Two examples

**Laura** is an employee earning COP 5,000,000 a month: COP 60,000,000 for the year, below the income threshold. But she pays for almost everything by credit card, and her card spending added up to COP 75,000,000. **She must file**, because of the credit card criterion.

**Andrés** earns COP 4,000,000 a month and owns an apartment with a tax value of COP 250,000,000, although he still owes the bank COP 180,000,000. The debt is subtracted to calculate net assets, but the threshold is measured on gross assets, and COP 250,000,000 exceeds COP 235,683,000. **He must file**, because of the assets criterion.

## And last year’s thresholds?

If you still haven’t filed the return due in 2026 (tax year 2025), the thresholds used the COP 49,799 UVT: **COP 224,095,500** in assets and **COP 69,718,600** for the other criteria. If your tax domicile is in an area affected by the August 2026 earthquake, check your date first: Decreto 1226 de 2026 moved some deadlines to October and November. If yours has passed, the sooner you file, the smaller the penalty.
